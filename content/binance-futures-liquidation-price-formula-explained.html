Crypto Futures Liquidation Price: Formula, Examples & Calculator

By CryptoCalc Pro • Updated October 2024 • 8 min read

Crypto futures trading offers enormous leverage — but one wrong move can wipe your position in minutes. Understanding exactly where you get liquidated before you open a trade is essential risk management. This guide breaks down the liquidation price formula used by Binance, Bybit, and OKX, with worked examples at multiple leverage levels.

What Is a Liquidation Price?

When you trade with leverage, the exchange lends you capital. To protect itself, it defines a liquidation price — if the market moves against you to that level, the exchange forcibly closes your position and keeps your margin as payment for the loan.

The Standard Liquidation Formula

Long Liquidation Price  = Entry × (1 − 1/Leverage + MMR)
Short Liquidation Price = Entry × (1 + 1/Leverage − MMR)

MMR = Maintenance Margin Rate (typically 0.4%–1%)

Risk Management Best Practices

  • Use a stop-loss well before your liquidation price — typically at 50%–75% of the distance to liquidation.
  • Never use more than 5x leverage without a clear risk management plan and position sizing rules.
  • Prefer isolated margin over cross margin to limit losses to the trade's margin, not your full account.
  • Monitor funding rates on perpetual contracts — high positive funding means longs pay shorts and can erode your margin over time.
