How to Calculate Crypto Tax in the US, UK & Australia (2024 Guide)

By CryptoCalc Pro • Updated October 2024 • 8 min read

Crypto tax is one of the most searched and most misunderstood topics in personal finance. Whether you're in the United States, United Kingdom, or Australia, this guide walks you through exactly how tax authorities treat your Bitcoin, Ethereum, and altcoin profits — with worked examples you can verify in our calculator.

The Universal Rule: Crypto Is Property

All three major English-speaking tax authorities classify cryptocurrency as a capital asset, not currency. This means every disposal — sale, swap, gift, or payment — is a potential taxable event.

UK Crypto Tax (HMRC 2024/25)

HMRC uses a Section 104 pool averaging method — your cost basis is the average cost of all units of a coin, not FIFO or LIFO. Gains above the £3,000 annual exempt amount are taxed at 18% (basic rate) or 24% (higher rate) from April 2024.

Australia Crypto Tax (ATO 2024)

The ATO treats crypto as CGT assets. Key rules: (1) Assets held >12 months get a 50% discount on the gain. (2) Mining, staking rewards, and airdrops may be ordinary income at receipt. (3) Crypto-to-crypto swaps are taxable disposals.

Step-by-Step Calculation Example

  1. Identify the disposal date and proceeds (market value in fiat).
  2. Subtract your cost basis (original purchase price + fees).
  3. Determine holding period (short- vs long-term).
  4. Apply the applicable tax rate from the tables above.
  5. Subtract any capital losses from the same tax year.

Example: You buy 1 ETH for \$1,500 and sell it 18 months later for \$3,200. Your gain = \$1,700. As a US taxpayer in the 22% bracket, you qualify for the 15% long-term rate, so your tax = \$255.
