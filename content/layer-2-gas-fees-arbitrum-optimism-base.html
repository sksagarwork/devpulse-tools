Layer 2 Gas Fees: Arbitrum vs Base vs Optimism (2024 Breakdown)

By CryptoCalc Pro • Updated October 2024 • 9 min read

High Ethereum mainnet gas fees have historically priced retail users out of DeFi. A simple Uniswap token swap during market volatility can easily cost \$15 to \$30 on L1. Thankfully, Ethereum Layer 2 rollups—boosted by EIP-4844 proto-danksharding—have permanently reduced fees to mere pennies. This guide breaks down live transaction costs across the major Layer 2 networks.

How EIP-4844 Slashed L2 Gas by 99%

Before March 2024, rollups had to post transaction data inside Ethereum calldata, which competed directly with every smart contract operation on mainnet. EIP-4844 introduced dedicated data blobs that exist for 18 days—long enough for fraud proofs to resolve—without polluting Ethereum long-term state. This eliminated the primary data bottleneck in scaling.

Which L2 Should You Choose?

  • Arbitrum One: Deepest liquidity, highest DeFi TVL (Total Value Locked), and largest ecosystem of yield protocols (GMX, Camelot, Uniswap).
  • Base: Fastest growing consumer ecosystem, seamless Coinbase app integration, and lowest fees for on-chain social apps.
  • Optimism: Foundation of the Superchain ecosystem, power-user friendly, and governance staking via OP token.

Frequently Asked Questions

Following the Dencun upgrade (EIP-4844), Base and Arbitrum consistently offer some of the lowest transaction fees in crypto, with token swaps averaging between $0.01 and $0.02, and simple transfers often under $0.005.

Blobs are temporary data storage units introduced on Ethereum L1 that allow Layer 2 rollups to post verification proofs cheaply without permanently occupying expensive Ethereum execution state. Blobs automatically purge after ~18 days.

Polygon PoS is technically an EVM-compatible sidechain with its own validator set, though Polygon is transitioning to zkEVM rollups that settle directly to Ethereum security.

You can bridge ETH and stablecoins using official native bridges (e.g., Arbitrum Bridge, Base Bridge) or third-party fast liquidity bridges like Across and Stargate. Alternatively, centralized exchanges like Bybit support direct withdrawals to Arbitrum and Base.
