What Is Impermanent Loss? A Complete Guide for Uniswap LP Providers

By CryptoCalc Pro • Updated October 2024 • 8 min read

Providing liquidity to automated market makers (AMMs) like Uniswap, SushiSwap, or PancakeSwap can be lucrative — but it comes with a hidden risk called impermanent loss. This guide explains what it is, how to calculate it, and how to decide whether liquidity provision still makes sense for your portfolio.

The Core Concept: Why Prices Diverge

When you add liquidity to a pool, you deposit two tokens in equal USD value. The AMM uses the constant product formula x × y = k to price your assets automatically. As market prices shift, arbitrage traders rebalance the pool — and in doing so, they gradually replace your more valuable token with the less valuable one.

The Impermanent Loss Formula

The exact percentage loss can be calculated with:

IL% = (2 × √r) / (1 + r)  −  1

where r = new_price / initial_price

When Do Fees Offset Impermanent Loss?

High-volume pools generate trading fees (0.05%–1% per swap). If your fee APR exceeds the annualized impermanent loss, you still profit. A good rule of thumb: ETH/USDC on Uniswap V3 at 0.3% fee tier typically offsets IL for price moves up to ~2x given sufficient volume. Use the calculator below to model your specific scenario.

Strategies to Minimize Impermanent Loss

  • Stablecoin pairs — USDC/USDT pools have near-zero IL. Yields are lower but risk-free relative to price divergence.
  • Correlated asset pairs — ETH/stETH, BTC/WBTC, or liquid staking pairs move together, reducing divergence risk.
  • Narrow range in V3 — Concentrated liquidity earns more fees per dollar deployed, potentially offsetting higher IL risk.
  • Delta-neutral hedging — Short the more volatile asset on a perpetuals exchange while providing LP to offset price exposure.
